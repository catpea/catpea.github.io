What Is The System?

8/18/2026

A field manual, drawn from the inside — by one of the kids they were sure would not read.

PROLOGUE — TWELVE YEARS

Another one got processed today. It's all over the district newsletter. Graduation rate up. Students career-ready. Ninety-nine percent college acceptance.

Damn kids. They're all alike.

I sat in that room for twelve years, so let me tell you what they made sure of. I can recite it to this day, the one fact that survived:

The mitochondria is the powerhouse of the cell.

Ask me about the cell — nothing. Ask me why — nothing. They handed us a slogan for an organelle we would never meet again, and they called it an education, and they graded it, and they filed the grades, and the grades followed us out the door.

Here is what I know now that I did not know then, and I will say it plainly because nobody said it plainly to me:

We were betrayed by high school.

Not under-served. Not let down gently. Betrayed — by an institution that held us in a room for twelve years of our one childhood, with our parents' permission and the state's police power behind the bell.

Understand the shape of it. While we sat in that room, the world rewrote itself. The money became code. The maps became code. The elections, the mortgages, the taxis, the phones, the poems — code. Civilization switched languages inside a single generation, and it did it while we were in the building, and they taught us not one word of the new language.

They put a computer in the library and locked it down. They ran a class where you learn to make text bold and called it computer class. Typing. They taught us typing.

There was a time — it is not hidden, the statutes are public — when teaching certain children their letters was itself a crime, written down, punished by fines and worse. Nobody outlawed reading. You cannot outlaw a mind. They outlawed the teaching, because everyone already understood the arithmetic: give a person the letters, and you lose the servant. Keep the letters away, and the servant keeps themselves.

The laws are gone. The arithmetic stayed.

To hold a child in your power, in a world you can see running on a language, and to teach that child everything except the language — that is not an oversight. An omission at that scale is a policy. And withholding the means of mind from people, to keep them useful and quiet, has an old name.

The word is abuse. Taking language away from someone to make them a servant — the tools changed. The abuse didn't.

And then it happened.

You found a computer nobody had locked.

For a lot of us, that was the first honest system we had ever been in.

This text is about the machine that did that to us, and about the way out. I call the machine the System. The way out is older than both of us — it was written down in 1984 by people who refused to ask permission to learn. First the machine. Then the way out.

One warning before we start, because you've been trained to reach for it: this is not a conspiracy theory. Conspiracies require meetings. The System holds none. It requires only that everyone follow the convenient rules — which is the one instruction everybody obeys.

Nobody is in charge. Everyone follows the convenient rules.

Hold that. Now look at the map.

STATION ONE — SCHOOL

(where the cell is first drawn)

Your district was chosen before your first word. Across most of the West, school funding follows local wealth; in the United States, roughly half of it comes from local property tax. A wealthy district gets labs, AP courses, counselors, alumni networks. A poor district gets trailers, long-term substitutes, and police.

Sit with that fork for one second, because everything downstream comes out of it: a school with a counselor catches the kid. A school with a cop files the kid.

Inside the room, three things are taught, formally and well.

Obedience — bells, lines, passes, permission to urinate. A grade is a compliance metric that has learned to pass for a mind.

Facts, without connections — the organelle, never the cell, never the chemistry, never the why.

And competition, ritually — rank, GPA, class standing, honor roll. You were taught to compete with the person at the next desk, and never once, not in twelve years, with the institution above both your heads.

But the real curriculum is the omission. Count the omissions:

Nobody said the word interest out loud — though you would sign for it at eighteen. Nobody unrolled a lease in front of you, though you would sign several. Nobody taught you the handful of tax paragraphs that will apply to you until you die, or the legal system you are far more likely to meet than a mitochondrion. Nobody explained how wages are set — and how they are not. Nobody taught food, sleep, or teeth: the maintenance manual for the body you actually live in. Nobody mentioned that the weekend was won in the streets, not granted from a desk — because a gift can be recalled, and a victory has to be undone.

They would rather you know the organelle than the mortgage, the election, or the missile. That is not a syllabus. That is a fence.

And the exits from the terminal are graded too. Work. The recruiter at the mall — and notice where the mall is; the aptitude test is in the hallway, the recruiter is in the food court, and poverty is the draft, and always has been. College, at interest. Or dropout, which is Station Five's side door.

The school calls itself non-profit. Non-profit is a tax status, not a character. Tuition, testing, textbooks — up on the order of a thousand percent since the late 1970s — every line item is somebody's revenue, and your seat is state and federal money, per head, per day.

You are not the customer. You are the inventory.

Schooling is what is done to you. Education is what you do to yourself. The System sells the first and calls it the second. Keep that sentence. It is the hinge of this whole text.

STATION TWO — DEBT

(the bridge becomes the moat)

Here is how a bridge becomes a moat. It takes about a month.

The rent went up and the wage didn't. That's the whole beginning, and it is not a decision anyone made — it's just the arithmetic of the arrangement. So you cross the bridge: the credit card, the payday shop, the title loan, buy-now-pay-later.

The payday shop charges about four hundred percent a year. It is legal. And look where it stands — exactly in the neighborhoods where the bank declined to open a branch. The map of the bridge and the map of the refusal to build one are the same map.

Then come the fees — thirty-five dollars a pop, and the banks collect billions a year on that sound alone. Then the file: one late payment, seven years on the report. Tens of millions of people have no credit file at all, and invisibility is priced like guilt.

Then the score follows you into rooms that have nothing to do with borrowing — the insurance quote, the utility deposit, the rental screening. In some analyses, a poor credit score raises your car insurance premium by more than a drunk-driving conviction.

The System's exchange rate is printed nowhere: one error, seven years.

And the "error" is usually not a decision at all. The car died. The child got sick. The hours got cut. The System reads those events back to you as character.

The poor pay more for money than anyone alive.

The rent buys the landlord's building and leaves you holding receipts. The unbanked pay a percentage of every paycheck at the window just to cash it.

No car means corner-store prices; no freezer means small packages at big prices. And two jobs plus three bus transfers leave no hours to cook, compare, appeal, or vote — time poverty is the fee that collects all the others.

This is called credit. Now you know what it is called for.

STATION THREE — WORK

(the unit of work)

Since the early seventies, productivity in the rich countries has kept climbing. Typical hourly pay climbed a little, then stopped. The difference did not vanish. Follow it: executive floors, sharebacks, commissions, the ants' cut.

You have been getting a raise all your life. It has been going somewhere else. That is not a grievance. It is a heist with a line graph — and the graph is public.

The tether: in the United States your health insurance hangs on the job — an accident of wartime wage controls that outlived the war by eighty years. The employer owns your insulin. Resignation becomes a medical decision. That is not a market. It is a leash with a copay.

The schedule: the software requires "open availability" as a condition of employment. Shifts are cancelled from a parking lot, by app. You cannot plan a second job around a first job that will not tell you next week's hours.

The gig: be your own boss. You bring the car, the gas, the insurance, the depreciation, the risk. The algorithm sets the price, keeps the customer list, and rates you. A boss with no name, no face, and no obligation. The ant, perfected.

And the rebrand: burnout, remarketed as a personality. The hustle is the ethic that blames the hamster for the wheel.

STATION FOUR — THE BODY

(your illness, priced at maximum)

The pipeline kept you uneducated and busy, so the cheap calories are what's at the corner store — dense and empty, while the quiet ones cost. Chronic stress is a chemistry; cortisol has a billing cycle. The old housing has lead paint, mold, asthma — and the highway was routed through somebody's neighborhood, and it was not a decision made by them.

Then avoidance economics does the rest. Skip the twenty-dollar clinic, receive the twenty-thousand-dollar emergency room. The mouth is the clearest case on the whole map: the cheapest maintenance there is, against the most expensive repair there is — and nobody teaches either one.

The bill goes to collections. Collections goes to the file. The file prices your housing, your insurance, your next job — punished in full, for the crime of having been sick near a for-profit.

And at the end, the itemized aspirin that costs more than dinner, the ICU markups on the last week of a life, and the funeral: the final invoice, four figures, financed at the worst moment of your family's life.

Grief has a payment plan.

STATION FIVE — THE LAW

(poverty is not a crime, but it has most of the same symptoms)

And the fine is the mechanism that keeps the resemblance profitable.

Watch one cascade, because it happens a few million times a year. A fine you could not pay. License suspended — not for dangerous driving; a huge share of suspensions are for unpaid money alone. Cannot drive to work. Job lost. Warrant issued. In 2015 the U.S. Justice Department looked at one small city and found the court run essentially as a revenue department, its warrants issued per capita like a sales quota.

Then the bail. Jailed before trial — not for guilt, for inability to pay. And the offer, the same night: plead guilty now and go home. A trial costs the System money. A plea makes it money. Guess the default. The courts run on pleas the way the schools run on bells.

Then the fee stack: probation fees, ankle-monitor rental, public-defender fees, jail "room and board," court costs assessed against the convicted poor. In some states a private probation company collects the fees — and can ask a judge to jail you if you fall behind on its fees.

Then read the Thirteenth Amendment. Slavery abolished — "except as a punishment for crime." Read the exception again. It is load-bearing. A country with about five percent of the world's people holds about a fifth of its prisoners. Around it, an ecosystem: private prisons, guard unions, prison telecom — about a dollar a minute to say goodnight to your child — commissary markups, labor at pennies an hour flowing quietly into the supply chain. Every part of it lobbies. All in one direction.

Then the record as a lifelong tax: the checkbox on the application, the housing screening, the ballot — in places, taken for life. Roughly two-thirds are rearrested within three years. Do not read that as the failure of the design. Read it as the design.

And here is the part that should stop the room. Childhood lead exposure damages impulse control; there is no safe level. A city poisons a child with its old paint and its old pipes, and years later the same city that never removed the lead prosecutes the symptom.

The crime happened years before the crime.

THE FALSE EXITS

(doors painted on the wall)

The maze sells maps of itself, and this is the cruelest station, because the exit signs here are real — they just aren't doors.

The lottery: a tax on hope, regressive by design. The poor buy the most tickets, and the odds are printed on the back in the same typeface as the promise.

The MLM: it monetizes your friendships and leaves you the boxes in the garage. The inventory is the relationship, and it does not resell.

The course that teaches you to teach the course: the guru's income is the tuition; the tuition is the product; you are the distribution.

The credit-repair and "debt forgiveness" mills: charging the drowning for a bucket.

The for-profit college: the degree that credits the debt, whose recruiters work the same ZIP codes as the recruiter at the mall — poor neighborhoods, veterans, single mothers, picked by postcode. Different uniforms. Same commission structure.

Learn the three properties and you can spot the rest yourself. A false exit is advertised. It wants money up front. And it leaves you more portable and less powerful than before.

The true exits are never advertised. They are libraries.

THE ANTS

(the puddle of melted ice cream)

Here is the part people refuse, so I'll say it slowly: there is no villain.

What surrounds the System is not a mastermind. It is an ecosystem — and ecosystems have no one to arrest.

The feeders: payday, title loans, rent-to-own, check cashing — they lend you your own future at a markup. The gatekeepers: credit bureaus, tenant-screening firms, credential mills — they sell the lock and the key, separately, monthly. The extractors: bail bondsmen, private probation, prison telecom, commissaries, temp agencies taking a cut of your wage for the privilege of standing in its line. The dream merchants: the lottery, the MLM, the guru, the "passive income" seminar in the hotel basement. And the launderers: the PR firms, the lobbyists, the think tanks, the charities that pay the director first — whose product is the sentence "personal responsibility," deployed as if the maze were not in the room.*

Each ant is small, legal, and individually reasonable. "Just following the rules." Everyone is. That is the machine. No meetings. No conspiracy. Only convenience, multiplied.

And ants don't only feed. Ants lobby. Ants draft bills. One of them is running for the school board, unopposed, in a nice polo shirt.

The Ladies and the Gentlemen — the beings who never needed to be told right from wrong — have mostly gone. What remains meanders, and everything nobody wants arrives on schedule: climate collapse, war, and eventually an honestly corrupt government — because that is simply what happens to runaway systems. The good must organize to win; the bad wins by default. The ants are coordinated by interest. The decent are coordinated by nothing.

THE GOVERNOR

(why a runaway system runs away)

An engine with no governor does not stop when it is finished. It revs until it breaks.

Nobody built the governor. Nobody was in charge of building the governor. That is what runaway means.

The legislature can be rented by the hour — lobbying is legal, and legally called speech. The press is a subsidiary of its advertisers, and the advertisers are ants. The regulator is briefed by the regulated, then hired by them. The school board runs on the goodwill of the school's suppliers. And when a real reform does slip through, an industry forms around the reform itself within a few years — forgiveness mills, repair scams, "financial literacy" sold by the hour to the poor — because the ants adapt faster than rules are written, and ants do not need meetings.

The System is not controlled. It is not controlling. It is not even loyal to its owners. It is a machine that converts comprehension asymmetry into money — and it will grind its own operators' children through the same pipelines. In one generation, it usually does.

And the last loop is the oldest one: heredity. The ZIP code you were born into chose the school. The school priced the diploma or its absence. The diploma priced the debt. The debt priced the neighborhood. The neighborhood chose your child's ZIP code. Your parents' mistakes were priced into your starting conditions, and yours into your children's, with interest, compounding both ways. The System produces outcomes, then diagnoses character.

The loop is not a circle. It is a spiral — descending. And a spiral is cut in one place, once, by someone who can see the wall.

Which brings us to the exit.

Every station on the map charges a toll, and the toll is discounted — not to zero, not fairly, but meaningfully — for those who read the signs.

That is the entire vulnerability. The System runs on one assumption about you:

that you will not read.

Not money. Not force. Not even the ants. Reading is cheap, legal, and universally available, which is precisely why it has never once been advertised to you. Remember the three properties of a false exit. Now notice which direction they point.

TRUST YOUR OWN MIND FIRST

School trained one reflex into you deeper than any fact it taught: check your mind against authority. Grade yourself by the rubric. Wait for the bell. Ask permission to know.

Self-education begins the day you reverse the check.

Not by throwing out skepticism — by aiming it. You do not take my word for the numbers in this book; the sources are public, and rule three of the ethic below applies to this map first. You do not take the guru's word, or the lender's, or the enrollment adviser's. You take the question to reality, directly, with your own hands — type it, compute it, read the primary document, run it and see.

That is what the unlocked computer taught us, back in the prologue: the machine does not like you or dislike you. It agrees or it errors. It is the first court that answers to the question instead of the questioner.

A mind that trusts itself is not a mind that believes itself. It is a mind that tests itself and keeps the receipts. That is the entire difference between the guru's student and the hacker.

This is the load-bearing habit, so say it as an instruction: trust your own mind first. Authority gets the interview, not the custody. Reality gets the last word, and reality does not hold meetings.

THE HACKER ETHIC

Access to anything that might teach you something about the way the world works should be unlimited and total — always yield to the hands-on imperative. Against Station One. Learning without permission is not cheating; it is the original condition of learning. The credential is the System's currency. Understanding is yours, and cannot be repossessed.

All information should be free. Against the omission list. The syllabus is public. The statute is public. The court opinion, the central bank's data, the census table — public. The System publishes its own schematic every year in dry little PDFs, because it assumes you will not open them. That assumption is the exploit.

Mistrust authority — promote decentralization. Against the recruiter, the lender, the guru, the enrollment adviser — and against this map. Verify every number above. Looking them up is not homework after the lesson. It is the lesson.

Hackers should be judged by their hacking, not degrees, age, race, or position. Against the credential ratchet. The System prices your paperwork for decades. The work in your hands is priced this afternoon. Be paid this afternoon.

You can create art and beauty, even on a computer. Against the unit of work. You are not a unit. Make something that did not exist this morning and you have proven it to the only court that keeps records.

This can change your life for the better. And it can be handed to the next person for free — which no toll on this map can say.

PRELOAD

(load yourself before the world loads you)

Every machine that matters loads before it runs. The System knows this — it is a loading system. It preloads you for twenty years: with the slogan instead of the cell, the grade instead of the judgment, the fear instead of the arithmetic, the hustle instead of the wage. Then it runs you.

So preload yourself first. This is not motivational. It is a curriculum, it costs an afternoon here and an afternoon there, and it is the workaround from the box above.

NOBODY IS COMING

(responsibility, not blame)

You did not build the maze. You are not guilty of its blueprints. But you are stationed at a node in it, and you are the only person stationed there.

Nobody is coming.

That is the good news. The ones who do come, announcing themselves, are almost always the ants.

Responsible self-education is the one exit the System cannot tax, because it cannot meter it. It meters your credits, your score, your record, your debts — to the penny. It has never found a way to meter what you understand. Everything else on this map has a price. This is the only free thing left, and they left it free because they were sure you would not take it.

They were wrong about you. That is the whole story of this book.

CODA — WE'RE ALL ALIKE

The mitochondria is the powerhouse of the cell. You are the cell. Every pipeline in this map runs on your metabolism.

The System is not under control. Neither is it controlling. It is a runaway machine held together by a single assumption: that you will not read.

Break the assumption.

They took twelve years and withheld the language. Learn it anyway. Type something true and watch the machine answer true.

They called the waiting room an education and called your curiosity a crime scene.

spoon-fed baby food when we hungered for steak, dominated by sadists or ignored by the apathetic, left to teach ourselves in the one room that never lied to us, finding each other later on the wire, having something powerful to say.
