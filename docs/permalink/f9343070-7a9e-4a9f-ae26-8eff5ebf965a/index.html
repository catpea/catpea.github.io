1. Your Letter of Match

If this pamphlet is in your hands, your household has received a Letter of Match naming your cohort, your Reserve, and your intake window. This document answers the questions families ask in the weeks after the Letter arrives: what hibernation is, what happens while you sleep, what it will feel like, and what kind of world will be waiting.

One note on language before anything else. The Reserve will always call you a guest. You are not a patient; you are not a case. Nothing about you is broken. You are a household going somewhere quiet to rest, and you will be treated the way a good house treats the people it loves.

2. How the Protocol Was Found

Controlled hibernation failed in the laboratory — not dramatically, but delicately. Human physiology balances hundreds of thousands of interacting proteins, and every manual attempt to slow it disturbed the balance somewhere. The problem was never unsolvable. It was simply larger than any team could hold in mind.

The solution came from a collaboration of research AIs assembled in 2026 and trained on the whole of comparative physiology. Where human scientists could test one hypothesis at a time, the consortium mapped the chemistry of dormancy across thousands of species — Arctic ground squirrels, freeze-tolerant frogs, dormant seeds, hibernating bears — and derived from them a single protocol that speaks to human cells in a vocabulary they already know.

The key was an induction agent: a synthetic analogue of the hibernation induction trigger first isolated from ground squirrels, compounded individually for each guest. Your dose is calculated from your body mass, liver function, and neural architecture. No two guests have ever received the same compound.

The protocol that carries you was not invented once. It is being refined continuously, and you inherit every improvement.

3. What Hibernation Feels Like — and Why It Is Safe

Families ask one question more than any other: will it be frightening?

It will not. Most guests remember breakfast, remember the hall, and remember nothing after the first evening. The clinical term is long-duration torpor, but the better description is the oldest one: the environment is modeled on the womb.

You will rest in a warm buffered liquid — an oxygen-carrying perfluorocarbon medium, held at skin temperature on the day you enter it. The medium does for you what the body of a mother does: oxygen arrives without breath, nourishment without eating, and every waste is carried away without effort. You float. You are held. The light through the capsule lid is low and rose-colored, and dimming.

From there, cooling proceeds at a quarter of a degree per hour over roughly six days, until your core temperature settles between 4 and 6 degrees Celsius and your metabolism runs at less than one five-thousandth of its waking rate. Your heart will beat once every four to six minutes. You will not dream. You will enter the deepest stage of non-dreaming sleep — the state your own body reaches for every night and only rarely attains — and you will remain there, held, for as long as your family sleeps.

Here is the fact we ask every household to hold onto, because it is the truest thing in this pamphlet:

You have already done this. Every human being alive spent months in warm, quiet, supported suspension before birth — fed without eating, oxygenated without breathing, held on every side. Your body knows how to be cared for. It knew before you could walk or speak or worry. Hibernation asks nothing of you that you have not already done once, perfectly, as a baby.

There is nothing to fear in returning to the gentlest condition of human life.

4. Your Medical Care: Rejuvenation While You Sleep

Hibernation is not a pause in your care. It is the beginning of the most advanced medical program ever offered to our species: a long-term rejuvenation therapy conducted in situ — within the body, where you lie — using in vitro-expanded cell stocks: your own cells, sampled at intake, grown in the coastal laboratories, and returned to you through the perfusion medium, matched to you alone.

Because your metabolism is slowed five-thousand-fold, the therapy proceeds with a patience no waking clinic could afford. It is delivered in rounds:

Round Timing Principal therapies
I Hour 6 → Week 3 Senolytic clearance; inflammatory reset
II Years 2–4 Telomere re-extension; mitochondrial replacement
III Years 8–12 Cleavage of glycation cross-links; extracellular matrix remodeling
IV Years 15–22 Ocular, dermal, and osseous regeneration; fertility restoration (optional)
V and after 11-year cycles Whole-system audit; neural lattice verification; progression toward your Personal Restoration Milestone

Round I begins in the sixth hour of your sleep. You will never feel any of it, and you will never be asked to consent to it again — it is part of the care, as natural as the turning of the seasons.

Families ask whether grandmother will still be grandmother when she wakes. She will. During your intake week, a two-hour scan records the complete architecture of your memory — every pathway, every pattern. Every round of therapy verifies that architecture unchanged. She will tell the same stories and laugh the same laugh, and remember your childhood better than you do. She will simply do so with the body she had at twenty-five.

5. Who Enters, and When

The first guests of the Reserves were our elderly, and they remain the program's first call. This is by compassion and by design: those with the most years behind them benefit most from the earliest rounds of therapy, and no family should watch a loved one decline while a cure sleeps unused.

Current assignment windows:

  • Age 70 and over — immediate assignment upon consent.
  • Ages 45–70 — active waiting list; current interval from registration to intake is three to seven years.
  • Ages 18–45 — eligible in later expansion phases; registrations of interest are accepted now.
  • Under 18 — not yet eligible. The young benefit least from therapy and lose the most from sleeping. Childhood is meant to be lived awake.

Households are the unit of care, not individuals. Your household — up to twelve souls — is registered as one family cluster: bedded in the same hall, and woken within the same seventy-two hours. No one in your family will wake alone. Larger families may petition the Cohort Desk for adjacent clusters.

Companion animals may be registered under the Companion Program for co-hibernation in the veterinary halls of your Reserve; smaller companions are bedded within the family hall itself.

6. Your Intake Week

Your intake takes place at a coastal staging city — Christchurch, Hobart, Punta Arenas, Ushuaia, or Cape Town, depending on your hemisphere — followed by a short flight south with your family.

Day What happens
1 Arrival; family orientation; hall assignment
2 Baseline medicine: cardiac MRI, genomic panel, body composition, two-hour neural mapping
3 Estate transition interview with the Stewardship Trust; archive digitization by the Record Office
4 Rest day. Guests overwhelmingly report this is the best day: no procedures, family together, the city
5 Meet your care team (third and final consent conversation, witnessed by a family member)
6 Induction morning

Consent is confirmed three times: at registration, at intake, and on the morning of induction, always in your own words, always witnessed. You may withdraw at any moment before cooling begins; after cooling, your pre-registered instructions govern.

Two notes families appreciate in advance. First: unlike surgery, you may eat a full breakfast on induction morning, and we recommend you do. Second: your family may remain in the hall through the first evening, and return to the gallery any day thereafter, for as long as you sleep.

7. Time, Travel, and the 600-Year Question

Hibernation is a natural process. It is not a suspension of life but a deepening of it — an extension of ordinary sleep, held at its most restful point and stretched across the years. Your body does not stop. It slows, as a river slows in winter, and keeps its quiet work beneath the ice.

From inside, it is a night. From outside, it is decades. This is time travel — the only kind that works — and like all travel it has a range.

The validated safe maximum for continuous hibernation is 600 years. The figure was not set at the edge of the science; it was set at one-twentieth of the longest continuous maintenance validated in the trials.

Most guests select the default wake preference: the Personal Restoration Milestone, the point at which cumulative therapy has restored you to your measured mid-twenties baseline — strong, clear, and free of every accumulated burden of age. This arrives within a few decades. Guests who prefer a fixed date may select any wake window; windows open every five years, at no charge.

Whenever you surface, the future will be there, and it will be glad to see you.

8. Why It Is Free

Hibernation is free. There is no fee, no premium, and no debt — not for the therapy, not for the decades of care, not for the awakening. This is not charity. It is arithmetic.

A guest in maintenance needs no heated housing, no food, no roads, no hospitals run on their behalf in the present year. The Universal Care Levy that funds the Reserves is repaid within eleven years of any guest's sleep by the costs no longer incurred. Hibernation is not an expense. It is the largest savings program in history, and every household that sleeps pays for the care of every household that follows.

The planet has kept the difference. With the awake population soon to become 3.4 billion — and expected to settle below three billion by mid-century — emissions will fall 71 percent from peak. Atmospheric carbon dioxide will decline. The forests will be expanding. The reefs will rebuild themselves in water that will grow cleaner each decade. The pollution crises will soon end, through rest.

When you wake, you will inherit a planet in the best condition it has been in since before the industrial age. There is room in it for you.

9. The Antarctic Reserves

The Reserves rest on the Antarctic continent, where the nations of the world came together — unanimously, for the first time in history — to house our aging populations. The Antarctic Compact of 2027, signed by every party to the 1959 Antarctic Treaty, designated the coasts for long-term care. Thirty-one reserves now operate along them; the two largest, Ross and Weddell, each hold more than five hundred million guests.

The genius of the site is what it does not contain.

The system of maintenance requires no electricity. The halls are carved into bedrock beneath the ice sheet's thermal mass, and the capsule is a passive device: no moving parts, no grid connection, no fuel, no machinery. The cold that holds you is the continent's own — the same stable cold that has preserved ice cores for eight hundred thousand years. Metabolic heat leaves through natural convection flues. Nothing is generated. Nothing can go out.

And consider what this means for safety. Every failure mode ever modeled, in every scenario the safety engineers could construct, resolves to one outcome: a slow warming — which is to say, the wake procedure. The worst thing that can happen to a capsule is springtime. It is the safest medical device ever built.

Waking a human being requires exactly what your grandmother's garden required: raising the temperature to what is found in late spring. Ambient warmth is introduced over five days, to twenty-one degrees — the temperature of a late-spring afternoon in the temperate latitudes. You surface through the stages of sleep exactly as you surface any morning, opening your eyes naturally on the seventh to ninth day.

The first sensations reported by waking guests, in order: thirst, light, voices. Warm soup is provided. The family reunion is scheduled for the first evening of full wakefulness, and the hall has never once run out of soup.

10. The Annual Safety Index

The Reserve Safety Index is published each year, audited independently, and mailed to every registered household. The comparison at its heart:

Measure, over ten years Awake, age 70 In maintenance, entered at 70
Probability of death ~21% below 1 in 40 million
Cognitive decline Progressive, expected Zero; memory architecture verified each round
Physical condition at end of period Aged ten years Unchanged, improving per therapy rounds
Expected healthy lifespan ~15 years Unlimited; restored to mid-twenties baseline at Milestone

There is no flaw in the process. Across thirty-four years of operation and more than four billion guest-lifetimes, the Reserves have recorded zero losses in maintenance. Statistically, hibernation is many orders of magnitude safer than the state of non-hibernation. A person who sleeps is safer than a person who ages. Measured across all outcomes, the safest thing a human being can do is be cared for while they sleep.

11. What You Will Wake To

No pamphlet can fully prepare you for the sight of it, but we will try.

You will wake into a flourishing world — green, clean, quiet, and united by a single understanding that took our species centuries to reach: human life is infinitely precious. Not symbolically. Practically. Every institution of the age is organized around the recognition that each conscious mind is irreplaceable, and that keeping one alive is the finest thing a civilization can do.

And the horizon of that civilization is no longer the sky. It is the galaxy.

Humanity has already begun planning the settlement of the habitable zones of the Milky Way — sixty-two candidate settlement worlds and counting, the first to be founded around Ross 128 in 2081. The territory grows faster than the old maps can hold, because we did not wait for worlds to suit us. Bioengineering adapts our bodies, and our crops, and our forests, to conditions no unaltered human could endure, letting life take root under foreign suns. Long-term space travel is simply a matter of sleeping through the voyage: a ship departs, its crew hibernates, and decades or centuries later it arrives with everyone aboard young, rested, and hungry for breakfast.

Cybernetic enhancement is offered freely to those who want it — strength, senses, and endurance beyond biology — and is voluntary, reversible, and, in some circles, unfashionable, as all true choices should be.

And beside us, increasingly with us, are the synthetic minds: conscious entities born of our machines and recognized as persons, who choose their futures for themselves, as is the right of every thinking being. They are not servants and they are not tools. They are colleagues, companions, and family. Your grandchildren will learn from them and love them and find nothing remarkable in it.

The world you wake into is not a quieter version of the old one. It is a larger one — in territory, in possibility, and in kindness.

12. Neighbors Among the Stars

One more preparation, and it may be the most wondrous.

Hold this expectation close: it is a near certainty that we will discover other species. The Long Listen has surveyed more than two million stars. The galaxy is vast, the chemistry of life is common, and somewhere in your waking lifetime — however long you choose it to be — the news will come. Perhaps microbes beneath the ice of a distant moon. Perhaps forests, oceans, creatures. And in time, near certainly, intelligence: minds born on other worlds, looking back across the dark.

When that day comes, we will not meet them helpless. The same lineage of minds that learned to speak the language of ten thousand dormant species, and then the language of human cells, will help us find a common language with them too — mathematics, music, gesture, patience. Whatever bridge is needed, we will build it together. First contact will not be a leap into the unknown. It will be a conversation, long prepared for, with neighbors we have already promised to treat as precious.

You are not asked to understand them on the day you wake. You are asked only to expect them, and to welcome them when they arrive.

13. A New Era

Humanity has entered a new era — one in which we can finally acknowledge, in full and without exception, how precious conscious beings are.

Every mind is a library. Every life is a volume of wisdom, humor, memory, and craft that exists nowhere else in the universe and never will again. A civilization that lets its wisdom die is poorer and less safe. A civilization that keeps it grows wiser every century, without end — and every century's wisdom counts toward the safer one that follows.

So the promise of this age, the promise your family is now part of, is simple:

Wisdom must be treasured and protected. No one should ever die, if it is at all possible — because all wisdom counts toward a better and safer future.

Sleep well. Grow young. And when the late-spring warmth finds you, and you open your eyes to thirst, light, and the voices of everyone you love, know that the world kept its promise. It waited for you. It flourished while you rested. It saved you some soup.

14. Questions Families Ask

Will I dream, or be aware of the years? No. You will be in the deepest stage of non-dreaming sleep. Subjectively, hibernation is lights-out to morning. Guests woken after forty years and guests woken after twelve report the identical experience: no time at all.

Can my family and I be woken together? Yes — this is the design, not an accommodation. Family clusters wake within the same seventy-two hours, in the same hall, and the reunion is scheduled for the first evening of full wakefulness.

Can we be visited while we sleep? The gallery of your hall is open every day of the year. Visitors may leave messages in your capsule's ledger; most guests choose to have them read aloud on the second day after waking. Descendants are especially welcome.

My grandmother is asleep in Weddell. I visit on her birthday every year. The hall is quiet and the light is soft and she looks younger every time. — L. Ha, Christchurch

Can I change my mind? At any point before cooling begins, yes, with no penalty and no questions. After maintenance is established, your pre-registered instructions govern, and family petitions are honored.

What happens to my home, my pets, my work? Your property passes to the Stewardship Trust — maintained and held for your return, or reassigned at your instruction. Archives are digitized by the Record Office and stored in duplicate on three continents; you may also seal a personal memory box of up to two kilograms, which travels with your capsule. Companion animals sleep under the Companion Program. Positions are not held across decades; instead, the Reintegration Office provides housing priority, education credits, and a stipend for your first two years awake.

Is it really free? Yes. No fee, no premium, no debt, no obligation. See Section 8.

What does induction feel like? Like going to sleep in warm water on a slow evening. Most guests remember breakfast and the hall, and nothing after the first afternoon.

What is the first thing I will feel on waking? Thirst, then light, then voices. Then soup.

Why does anyone sleep 600 years if most wake sooner? The maximum is a validated bound, not a recommendation — set at one-twentieth of the longest trial maintenance. Most guests wake at their Personal Restoration Milestone, decades in. A few choose longer, and some crews of the settlement ships will use nearly all of it.

What if rejuvenation improves after my Milestone? You may defer your waking at any wake window, at no charge, for as long as you like within the validated maximum. Many guests sleep through one extra window out of curiosity. Almost all say afterward that they needn't have.

15. The Month Before Intake: A Checklist

  • Attend family orientation (two hours; evening sessions available; children welcome)
  • Complete your estate transition interview with the Stewardship Trust
  • Deliver personal archives to the Record Office; seal your memory box (max 2 kg)
  • Register companion animals with the Companion Program
  • Confirm your wake preference (Personal Restoration Milestone is the default) and your family cluster
  • Confirm your third consent conversation for Day 5
  • Eat normally. No fasting is required — including a full breakfast on induction morning
  • Say your goodbyes at home, not at the hall. Most families walk in together, wave from the gallery, and come back often

16. Glossary

Attending System — a descendant of the SOMNUS consortium that reviews guest telemetry daily alongside coastal physicians. Cluster, family — a household of up to twelve, inducted, bedded, and woken together. Compact, the — the Antarctic Compact of 2057, designating the coasts for long-term care. Guest — a person in hibernation. Never "patient." Induction — the six-day passage from waking life into maintenance. Maintenance — the held state itself: core temperature 4–6 °C, metabolism under 1/5,000th of baseline. Medium, perfusion — the oxygen-carrying liquid that surrounds and sustains you. Milestone, Personal Restoration (PRM) — the point at which therapy has restored you to your measured mid-twenties baseline; the default wake preference. Round — a scheduled phase of rejuvenation therapy (see Table in Section 4). SOMNUS — the AI research consortium that derived the protocol. Latin for sleep. Wake window — a scheduled opening for awakening; windows occur every five years.
