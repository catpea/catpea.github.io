Music Label development update, September 13, 2026.

Our little label now has three artists with distinct lives, a world you can browse, and three real recordings you can play. The latest development pass focused on making that work dependable and easier to explore. This is our first spoken project update. First, the recent news. Then, what still needs attention. After that, there is an optional tour for anyone meeting the project for the first time.

The new roster is Brooklyn Cowboy, Rat Handsome, and Cat Magic. Their biographies, musical directions, album plans, and first week of stories were written through the working Codex adapter, with separate model reviews. Across the three artists, there are thirty planned albums and one hundred and twenty song intentions. Three of those intentions have become actual recordings.

Brooklyn Cowboy's first song is The Last Bus Knows My Name. Rat Handsome sings Make Room for Vera. Cat Magic brings us Captain of the Biscuit Tin. Each recording is roughly two minutes long. The local song-maker produced the audio, and the listening room brings together playback, lyrics, downloads, and the full catalog. You can copy that folder and open it without running a server.

The most recent repairs protect work that has already succeeded. Selected lyrics now stay exactly the same when a recording is made. If a review is interrupted, its draft is retained. If audio generation finishes but conversion fails, the finished audio can be used to complete the recording without generating the music again. Requests for several songs by the same artist now process every selected song.

The listening room also has clearer download names, accurate playback lengths, and labels showing whether a take is awaiting a decision. Desktop and mobile browser checks confirmed playback and a layout that fits the screen. The original supplied world, the new artist world, and all retained lyrics and recordings survived this review unchanged.

The latest recorded offline test run passed all one hundred and fifty-four checks. The broader acceptance report records ninety-three of ninety-five criteria as passed. Two still need live evidence.

Here is what comes next, and what is still missing.

The three recordings need direct listening and an artistic assessment of the singing, style, intelligibility, and endings. Automated transcription recovered their chorus hooks, and the files decode and play. Those checks leave musical judgment open. Brooklyn Cowboy's ending deserves particular attention because the take reached its two-minute limit and its final words were uncertain in transcription. All three remain first takes awaiting a listening decision. None has been approved for release or published.

The other missing demonstration is a real notification reaching a real inbox with a working listening link. The email clients and local capture tests exist, but no authorized live sender and recipient have been exercised. That remains a specific external test.

The packaged application has been tested on Linux. Windows, macOS, and the intended older Node runtime still need their own checks. More recordings and a more advanced music service are possible future steps. The replaceable adapter gives us room to improve production while keeping the artists and their history.

That ends the development news. The rest is a tour of the program and its features. Returning listeners can stop here and come back for the next update.

Music Label is a small text-adventure game about running a fictional record label called Star Records. You enter a populated creative world, browse its people and plans, and guide what they become.

Brooklyn Cowboy makes 1990s country about wisdom, power, and growing up. Rat Handsome brings traditional pop and swinging workout music for warrior ladies. Cat Magic plays bluegrass for hiking and adventure, with trail advice and cute, funny animal warfare expressed as harmless woodland mischief.

You can explore artists, albums, and song ideas through a keyboard-friendly terminal or browser. The listening room lets you hear completed candidates and read their lyrics. A calendar shows planned work. With the configured model adapter, you can ask for new creative development in ordinary English and advance the world deliberately, keeping coherent scenes and earlier history.

The world's memory lives in a portable, readable text save. It keeps identities, descriptions, selected memories, dates, decisions, and instructions. Lyrics and recordings are separate files kept alongside that world. Browsing saved work needs no model connection, and continuing it does not depend on an old chat being available.

Outside tools receive defined work requests and return results tied to the right song and source. You can approve or reject a particular recording and express preferences for future work. Approval belongs to that exact take. Private listening views and public views serve different audiences, and public release requires evidence that an approved recording was actually published. The browser, email clients, and feeds all work from the same world.

The focus remains the small label, its characters, and a reliable path from an idea to a recording you can hear.
