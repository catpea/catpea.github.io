Appositional Emphasis Technique - Behavioral Science Section, Interview and Negotiation Training Group

9/15/2026

FIELD BULLETIN NO. 26-114

ISSUING UNIT: Behavioral Science Section, Interview & Negotiation Training Group DISTRIBUTION: ALL SWORN PERSONNEL, ALL FIELD OFFICES. RETAIN WITH FIELD MANUAL, VOL. IV (NEGOTIATED COMPLIANCE). SUBJECT: AUTHORIZATION AND STANDARDIZATION OF THE APPOSITIONAL EMPHASIS TECHNIQUE (AET) CLASSIFICATION: FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY — TRAINING BULLETIN EFFECTIVE: IMMEDIATELY

1. PURPOSE. This bulletin authorizes field use of the Appositional Emphasis Technique (hereinafter "AET") and establishes minimum proficiency standards. The AET is a non-contact emphasis posture for use during custodial interviews, threshold contacts, administrative proceedings, and any negotiation in which the operator wishes a statement to be received as both generous and final. The AET is silent, portable, and requires no equipment. (No fiscal impact.)

2. BACKGROUND. In a multi-agency review of recorded interviews, analysts observed that the most effective negotiators shared a single unconscious posture: fingertips joined at the midline of the body. Subjects exposed to the posture exhibited elevated compliance markers, reduced volubility, and an increased willingness to say "okay" in a manner investigators describe as "settled."

3. TECHNIQUE DESCRIPTION. (a) The operator joins the tips of the index fingers — optionally index and middle — at or slightly above belt level. (b) The palms incline toward one another but do not touch. The remaining fingers curl inward toward the palm, forming what training materials term "the soft clutch." (c) Elbows unlocked. Shoulders level. Facial expression neutral and patient. (d) The operator may rock the joined fingertips against one another once per declarative sentence. One contact signifies patience. Two contacts signify a generosity that is ending. Sustained contact signifies that the operator has all day, and would prefer the subject did as well.

4. TACTICAL RATIONALE. The AET communicates three things simultaneously: (a) the operator's hands are empty; (b) the operator is being gentle; (c) the operator is being gentle on purpose. Perpetrators and assailants consistently interpret sustained apposition as evidence that resistance is being admired rather than considered. Seated subjects report the impression that the operator is "about to be reasonable," an impression they identify as the worst part. Field trials report fewer raised voices, fewer withdrawn statements, and a measurable increase in subjects requesting to "just start over."

5. DEPLOYMENT. (a) CUSTODIAL INTERVIEW. Deploy at the moment the perpetrator says "I want my lawyer." Maintain apposition through "you got nothing on me." (b) THRESHOLD CONTACT AT A SUBJECT'S DOMICILE. Deploy at belt height, hands forward of the body, so the silhouette reads "courteous" from within the doorway and "settled" from without. (c) The AET shall not be deployed while holding documents, coffee, or a radio. The empty hand is the message.

6. PRACTICE SCENARIOS. Operators will rehearse until reflexive. (a) ADMINISTRATIVE (PROMOTION REQUEST). Stand two paces before the reviewing officer. State the request in one sentence. On the phrase "my record speaks," join the fingertips. If the officer replies "we'll see," apposition will be maintained until the officer looks down at the file. The gesture will do the rest. (b) JUDICIAL (STATEMENT UNDERLINING). When delivering a statement intended to carry, join the fingertips at the operative clause. Testimony so underlined has been observed to ring beyond the courtroom, into the hallway, and down the courthouse steps. (c) NEGOTIATED ENTRY. Subject within a domicile refuses to open the door. Operator deploys the AET at chest height, visible through the gap, and states the terms slowly.

7. PERFORMANCE NOTE. If the subject mirrors the posture, break apposition immediately and withdraw two steps. A subject who mirrors the AET has been trained.

8. NOMENCLATURE. The Nomenclature Review Board has not yet issued a formal term. One early submission — citing "a certain resemblance to the feet of a pet parakeet placed, in a friendly manner, upon its back" — was struck from the record for insufficient gravity. The reviewer has been reassigned.

9. FIELD NOTE. The technique predates this bulletin and has already escaped the training environment, where it has been observed and independently named by young civilians with no law enforcement affiliation.

On the street, the name for this powerful technique is: "bird hands."
