Wisdom #2372

Forty Thousand Winters

Through ritualistic handmarks and lyrical prose, this piece weaves together themes of cultural preservation, ancestral responsibility, and the timeless human need to transmit wisdom across generations. The seven reminders echo the seven marks of the Löwenmensch figurine, a 35,000–41,000-year-old zoomorphic sculpture carved from mammoth ivory using a flint stone knife. This ancient artifact—one of humanity's oldest artistic representations—becomes a metaphor for how human consciousness has always sought to merge the animal and the human, to honor the wild within ourselves, and to leave enduring marks upon the world. Drawing on indigenous spiritual traditions and deep time, this piece explores how remembrance and ceremony anchor communities, how respect for nature sustains survival, and how each person carries within them an ancient knowing. A haunting reminder that we are both carriers and keepers of what came before, obligated to pass the sacred forward to those who will come after.

La Fuente de la Juventud (The Fountain of Youth)

A dying conquistador's deathbed confession reveals the Fountain of Youth. Through vivid memories of exploring La Florida, he discovers that genuine youth lies not in physical vitality but in curiosity, laughter, affection, and freedom from permission. A profound meditation on what it truly means to remain young at heart.

Together they form a poetic snapshot of modern philosophy’s journey from perception to being.
