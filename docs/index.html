Featuring posthumous letter from a dead contrarian writer, who answers the young manifesto's author as a fellow witness. He refuses the halo of sainthood, argues that the future is a creditor sending invoices to young people, and separates a diploma, which is a receipt for time served, from an education, which is a weapon that must be looted from libraries and argument. He turns the critique of religion into a critique of every artificial scarcity, from grace rationed by clergy to the means-test queue. The letter closes on the idea that anger is grief walking upright and that the rage and the love are the same muscle, so that the truth needs only a voice in time.
